\chapter{Fantasy Species Descriptors}\index{Fantasy Species Descriptors}

In a high fantasy setting, some GMs may want dwarves and elves to be mechanically different from humans. Below are some possibilities for how this might work.

\section{Variant Rule: Two Descriptors}

By having dwarf, elf, or other species take the place of a character’s descriptor, it creates a situation where only human characters have the variability of choosing a descriptor that suits their personality. The GM might instead allow all human characters to have two descriptors, and nonhuman characters to have a standard descriptor in addition to their species descriptor.

Sometimes contradictory descriptors might weaken or negate each other’s benefits and drawbacks. If one descriptor gives training in a skill and another gives an inability in that skill, they cancel each other out and the character doesn’t have any modifier for that skill at all.

\section{Descriptors As Species} 

If a player wants to play a nearly human species without any exceptional or unique special abilities, it’s easy for a GM to pick an appropriate descriptor and use it as that species’ descriptor. A greyhound-like species might have the Fast descriptor.

\section{Catfolk}\index{Species Descriptors!Fantasy Species Descriptors!Catfolk}

You are unmistakably feline. Your people have fur; large, pointed ears; sharp teeth and claws; and even tails. You are nimble, graceful, and quick. An ancient and sophisticated culture, your people have their own language, customs, and traditions developed in relative seclusion over the centuries. Neither conquerors nor conquered, the success of your society has come from the fact that you have given most others a wide berth. As a people, you almost never get involved in wars or similar matters, which has given other cultures the idea that you are aloof, unapproachable, or mysterious. As long as they leave you alone, what they think is fine with you. 

You gain the following characteristics: 

\textbf{Agile}: +4 to your Speed Pool.

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in climbing and balance tasks. 

\textbf{Bared Claws}: Even unarmed, your claws are light weapons that inflict 4 points of damage. 

\textbf{Initial Link to the Starting Adventure}: From the following list of options, choose how you became involved in the first adventure. 

\begin{enumerate}
\item You were curious as to what the other PCs were up to. 

\item You needed to get out of town, and the PCs were going in the same direction as you. 

\item You are interested in making a profit, and the other PCs seem to have a lead on doing just that. 

\item It seemed like a lark.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Dragonfolk}\index{Species Descriptors!Fantasy Species Descriptors!Dragonfolk}

You have scales, fangs, claws, and magic—gifts of the dragons. You might have been born of dragonfolk parents, willingly transformed in a magical ceremony, or chosen by a dragon to be their agent or champion. You have a great destiny before you, but it is your choice whether to make it your own or bend to the will of those who made you what you are. Some people mistrust or fear you, and others consider you a prophet or wish to exploit your power for their own goals. 

You gain the following characteristics: 

\textbf{Sturdy}: +2 to your Might Pool. 

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in intimidation

\textbf{Dragonbreath} (3 Might points): You breathe out a blast of energy in an immediate area. Choose one type of energy (arcane, cold, fire, thorn, and so on); the blast inflicts 2 points of damage of this kind of energy (ignores Armor) to all creatures or objects within the area. Because this is an area attack, adding Effort to increase your damage works differently than it does for single-target attacks. If you apply a level of Effort to increase the damage, add 2 points of damage for each target, and even if you fail your attack roll, all targets in the area still take 1 point of damage. Action. 

\textbf{Draconic Resistance}: You gain +2 Armor against the type of energy you create with your dragonbreath. 

\textbf{Scaly}: +1 to Armor. 

\textbf{Inability}: You have difficulty relating to non-dragons. Tasks to persuade non-dragons are hindered. 

\textbf{Initial Link to the Starting Adventure}: From the following list of options, choose how you became involved in the first adventure.

\begin{enumerate}
\item You believe the other PCs can help you solve a mystery about your heritage. 
\item You needed to get out of town, and the PCs were going in the same direction as you. 
\item Your creator, master, or mentor told you to help the PCs. 
\item You want to make a name for yourself, and the other PCs seem competent and compatible.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Dwarf}\index{Species Descriptors!Fantasy Species Descriptors!Dwarf}

You’re a stocky, broad-shouldered, bearded native of the mountains and hills. You’re also as stubborn as the stone in which the dwarves carve their homes under the mountains. Tradition, honor, pride in smithcraft and warcraft, and a keen appreciation of the wealth buried under the roots of the world are all part of your heritage. Those who wish you ill should be wary of your temper. When dwarves are wronged, they never forget.

You gain the following characteristics:

\textbf{Stalwart}: +2 to your Might Pool.

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in Might defense rolls.

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in tasks related to stone, including sensing stonework traps, knowing the history of a particular piece of stonecraft, and knowing your distance beneath the surface.

\textbf{Skill}: You are practiced in using axes.

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in using the tools required to shape and mine stone. 

\textbf{Vulnerability}: When you fail an Intellect defense roll to avoid damage, you take 1 extra point of damage.

\textbf{Additional Equipment}: You have an axe.

\textbf{Initial Link to the Starting Adventure}: From the following list of options, choose how you became involved in the first adventure.

\begin{enumerate}
\item You found the PCs wandering a maze of tunnels and led them to safety.
\item The PCs hired you to dig out the entrance to a buried ruin.
\item You tracked down the thieves of your ancestor’s tomb and found they were the PCs. Instead of killing them, you joined them.
\item Before dwarves settle down, they need to see the world.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Elf}\index{Species Descriptors!Fantasy Species Descriptors!Elf}

You haunt the woodlands and deep, natural realms, as your people have for millennia. You are the arrow in the night, the shadow in the glade, and the laughter on the wind. As an elf, you are slender, quick, graceful, and long lived. You manage the sorrows of living well past many mortal lifetimes with song, wine, and an appreciation for the deep beauties of growing things, especially trees, which can live even longer than you do.

You gain the following characteristics:

\textbf{Agile}: +2 to your Speed Pool.

\textbf{Long-Lived}: Your natural lifespan (unless tragically cut short) is thousands of years.

\textbf{Skill}: You are specialized in tasks related to perception.

\textbf{Skill}: You are practiced in using one bow variety of your choice.

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in stealth tasks. In areas of natural woodland, you are specialized in stealth tasks. 

\textbf{Fragile}: When you fail a Might defense roll to avoid damage, you take 1 extra point of damage.

\textbf{Additional Equipment}: You have a bow and a quiver of arrows to go with it.

\textbf{Initial Link to the Starting Adventure}: From the following list of options, choose how you became involved in the first adventure.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Before putting an arrow in the forest intruders, you confronted them and met the PCs, who were on an important quest.
\item Your heart yearned for farther shores, and the PCs offered to take you along to new places.
\item Your home was burned by strangers from another place, and you gathered the PCs along the way as you tracked down the villains.
\item An adventure was in the offing, and you didn’t want to be left behind.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Gnome}\index{Species Descriptors!Fantasy Species Descriptors!Gnome}

You are curious and love discovering ways to turn found things into art, tools, or weapons. You might be a sculptor, smith, artist, chef, storyteller, or inventor. Alchemy, magic, and engineering fascinate you. Other beings may see you as a strange mix of a nature-loving elf and a craft-obsessed dwarf, but you and your kind are unique people with a passion for life, exploration, and creation. 

You gain the following characteristics: 

\textbf{Genius}: +2 to your Intellect Pool. 

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in two skills that suit your creative nature, such as alchemy, smithing, poetry, cooking, woodcarving, or pottery. 

\textbf{Skill}: You are practiced in using hammers. 

\textbf{Natural Affinity}: You gain one of the following abilities: Communication, Eyes Adjusted, or Minor Illusion. 

\textbf{Inability}: Your small size makes some physical tasks difficult. Might-based tasks are hindered. 

\textbf{Additional Equipmen}t: You have a bag of light tools or a bag of heavy tools.

\textbf{Initial Link to the Starting Adventure}: From the following list of options, choose how you became involved in the first adventure. 

\begin{enumerate} 
\item You think an object or material you’ve been looking for can be found where the other PCs are going. 
\item You were recruited because of your knowledge on a particular subject.
\item You were bored and it sounded like the PCs were going to do something interesting. 
\item You owe one of the PCs a favor for a useful gift in the past.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Half-giant}\index{Species Descriptors!Fantasy Species Descriptors!Half-giant}

You stand at least 12 feet (4 m) tall and tower over everyone around you. Whether you are a full-blooded giant or merely have giant heritage from large ancestors, you’re massive. Always large for your age, it became an issue only once you reached puberty and topped 7 feet (2 m) in height, and kept growing from there.

You gain the following characteristics: 

\textbf{Tough}: +4 to your Might Pool.

\textbf{Mass and Strength}: You inflict +1 point of damage with your melee attacks and attacks with thrown weapons.

\textbf{Breaker}: Tasks related to breaking things by smashing them are eased.

\textbf{Inability}: You’re too large to accomplish normal things. Tasks related to initiative, stealth, and fine manipulation of any sort (such as lockpicking or repair tasks) are hindered.

\textbf{Additional Equipment}: You have a heavy weapon of your choice.

\textbf{Initial Link to the Starting Adventure}: From the following list of options, choose how you became involved in the first adventure.

\begin{enumerate} 
\item You fished the PCs out of a deep hole they’d fallen into while exploring.
\item You were the PCs’ guide in the land of giants and stayed with them afterward.
\item The PCs helped you escape a nether realm where other giants were imprisoned by the gods.
\item You kept the PCs from being discovered by hiding them behind your bulk when they were on the run.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Halfling}\index{Species Descriptors!Fantasy Species Descriptors!Halfling}

Three feet tall and proud, you are fond of the comforts of home but itching for a little adventure now and then. Small and quick, you have a way of getting along with everyone. You might have been raised in a halfling village, a mixed community where humans and the small folk work and eat side by side as friends, or a less welcoming environment where your people get things done using deception and criminal activity. You and humans have a lot in common—you’re just more compact and efficient about it. 

You gain the following characteristics: 

\textbf{Agile}: +2 to your Speed Pool. 

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in pleasant social interactions. 

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in stealth. 

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in Intellect defense. 

\textbf{Advantage}: When you use 1 XP to reroll a d20 for any roll that affects only you, add 3 to the reroll. 

\textbf{Inability}: Your small size makes some physical tasks difficult. Might-based tasks are hindered. 

\textbf{Initial Link to the Starting Adventure}: From the following list of options, choose how you became involved in the first adventure. 

\begin{enumerate} 
\item You were fleeing someone and literally ran into the other PCs. 
\item You were invited (or invited yourself) as a good luck charm. 
\item You were tricked into going with the other PCs or were brought along despite your very reasonable objections. 
\item You’re very protective of another PC and want to make sure they get through the upcoming challenges.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Hellborn}\index{Species Descriptors!Fantasy Species Descriptors!Hellborn}

Demons of the underworld sometimes escape. When they do, they can taint human bloodlines. Things like you are the result of such unnatural unions. Part human and part something else, you are an orphan of a supernatural dalliance. Thanks to your unsettling appearance, you’ve probably been forced to make your own way in a world that often fears and resents you. Some of your kin have large horns, tails, and pointed teeth. Others are more subtle or more obvious in their differences—a shadow of a knife-edge in their face and a touch that withers normal plants, a little too much fire in their eyes and a scent of ash in the air, a forked tongue, goatlike legs, or the inability to cast a shadow. Work with the GM on your particular helborn appearance.

You gain the following characteristics. 

\textbf{Devious}: +2 to your Intellect Pool.

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in tasks related to magic lore and lore of the underworld.

\textbf{Fire Adapted}: +2 to Armor against damage from fire only.

\textbf{Hellborn Magic}: You are inherently magical. Choose one low-tier ability from the Abilities chapter. If the GM agrees it is appropriate, you gain that ability as part of your helborn heritage, and can use it like any other type or focus ability.

\textbf{Inner Evil}: You sometimes lose control and risk hurting your allies. When you roll a 1, the GM has the option to intrude by indicating that you lose control. Once you’ve lost control, you attack any and every living creature within short range. You can’t spend Intellect points for any reason other than to try to regain control (a difficulty 2 task). After you regain control, you suffer a –1 penalty to all rolls for one hour.

\textbf{Inability}: People distrust you. Tasks to persuade or deceive are hindered.

\textbf{Initial Link to the Starting Adventure}: From the following list of options, choose how you became involved in the first adventure.

\begin{enumerate}
\item You were nearly beaten to death by people who didn’t like your look, but the PCs found and revived you.
\item The PCs hired you for your knowledge of magic.
\item Every so often you get visions of people trapped in the underworld. You tracked those people down and found the PCs, who’d never visited the underworld. Yet.
\item Your situation at home became untenable because of how people reacted to your looks. You joined the PCs to get away.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Lizardfolk}\index{Species Descriptors!Fantasy Species Descriptors!Lizardfolk}

You are from a long line of fierce reptilian predators. You show your fangs and scales proudly. Your people survive and thrive in the wetlands, guarding their eggs, raising their hatchlings, and protecting their territory. City-builders may call you a savage and your culture primitive, but there is grace in your hunting, artistry in your crafting, joy in your songs, and reverence in your worship. 

You gain the following characteristics: 

\textbf{Agile}: +2 to your Speed Pool. 

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in balancing, jumping, and swimming. 

\textbf{Skill}: You are trained in hunting and tracking. 

\textbf{Skill}: You are practiced in using javelins and spears. 

\textbf{Scaly}: +1 to Armor. 

\textbf{Inability}: Your slightly clawed hands make fine detail work difficult. You have an inability with picking locks, picking pockets, and other manual dexterity tasks (but not crafting). 

\textbf{Additional Equipment}: You have a spear and a pair of javelins. 

\textbf{Initial Link to the Starting Adventure}: From the following list of options, choose how you became involved in the first adventure. 

\begin{enumerate}
\item The other PCs were lost in your territory and you were sent to escort them out. 
\item Something has been attacking your community and you want to find and destroy it. 
\item You were exiled from your community and need to prove your worthiness before you can return to it. 
\item You or your priest had a vision of you traveling with the other PCs.
\end{enumerate}
